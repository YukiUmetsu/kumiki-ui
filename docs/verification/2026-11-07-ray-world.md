## Second pass: realism

The owner judged the drop shape and the landscape not realistic enough. Both were real faults, and both came from the method.

| Fault | Cause | Fix |
|---|---|---|
| The drop had a spout, then read as a light bulb | It was sculpted by hand | `pendantModes` solves the Young-Laplace equation and fits it to two smooth shape modes. At this scale a drop stays nearly round, and it stretches only as it lets go |
| Rings and ribs inside the drop | A lookup table has kinks, and refraction magnifies them | Analytic modes are perfectly smooth |
| The picture in the drop was smeared | The lens mapping was a guess | Rays now map by exit angle, so the drop holds the whole place upside down, as a real one does |
| The rock read as pewter | Procedural noise | A scanned CC0 rock with colour, normal and height maps |
| The valley read as a game | Procedural terrain cannot pass as a real place | The world stays as an honest CG option. A film cut from real drone footage was tried, then dropped: it was mostly other people's camera work and showed little of this library |
| The ice cave read as computer graphics | It was a procedural tunnel | It is now a real photograph. It opens sharp, then focus pulls back to a drop on a ledge textured from the same picture |
| The background blur showed a grid | One mip level keeps its texels | A soft plate gathers nine taps across the aperture |
| The highlight on the drop was one small dot | A photograph clips its light source | The bright places get their range back, so they glow softly in the water |

Earlier in this pass, the film also gained the world scene type, 9:16 framing, named moments for sound, a limiter and 3200 px photographs.

## Drop against real photographs

## Release against real footage

The owner supplied a watermarked stock preview of a drop leaving a leaf and hitting water, as a reference only. None of its pixels are used, and it is not in this repository. Stepping through it frame by frame showed what the film lacked.

| Real footage | Film before | Film now |
|---|---|---|
| The drop sags into a pear slowly, then stalls | It stretched in 0.35 s | It stretches over 1 s and releases on the sound cue at frame 108 |
| The neck draws into a long thin thread, then snaps | A short neck that disappeared | A thread that thins and snaps |
| A small satellite droplet follows | None | Added |
| The rest recoils to a bead on the surface | The surface was left bare | Added, and it wobbles as it settles |
| Rings run outward from one impact, behind a front | Rings were already present everywhere | Rings start at one moment and spread |
| A bright crescent sits low in the drop | Already present | Unchanged |
| A free drop is a wobbling sphere | Already present | Unchanged |

The reference drop is small in its frame, and the film keeps a large drop on purpose, because the picture inside it is the idea. That difference is a choice.

## Rendering

Every earlier film render ran in software, because `film:render` passed no GPU flags and headless Chromium then draws WebGL with SwiftShader. `film:render --gl=gpu` uses the real GPU. Software stays the default, because its output does not depend on the machine.

| Measure | Software | `--gl=gpu` |
|---|---|---|
| The Drop, both shapes, 1,560 frames | About 80 minutes | 805 s |
| Same 90 frames rendered twice | Not measured | Identical SHA-256 |

## Open items

- **Human review is pending.** An agent looked at the frames. Nobody has approved the look.
- **`river-flight` is computer graphics.** It is a stylized option, not a realistic one.
- **The stories preview at 8 samples or fewer.** Slow GPUs will not play them in real time.
- **`The Plaster Valley` is a stylized model.** Its ice now has pale firn and layered blue cliffs, but it is the least real of the films.
- **Sound has not been heard.** Mix numbers are measured. The Plaster mix reports 4 possible clicks. Those measured as bursts inside the recordings, with no file start or stop at 3 of those points. Nobody has listened to them.
- **The Drop's mix uses 8.5 dB of limiting**, close to the 9 dB limit, so a louder cue could fail the gate.
- **The showcase films add 42 MB to the repository.** A maintainer should decide whether they belong in git.

# ARCH1 reference study: elevations measured from photographs and drawings (2026-11-17)

**Pack:** `architecture` (ARCH1). **Spec:** [architecture](../component-specs/architecture.md).
**Status:** agent study and agent review of renders. **Human visual review is not recorded.**

## Why

The first ARCH1 elevations had the right human scale (doors, storeys, stairs), but they were generic: plain boxes and
bands, with no shadow and no characteristic parts. This study compares each building type with photographs or period
drawings of real buildings. The proportions and parts seen there are turned into construction rules, and each rule is
checked in a close-up render.

## Method

1. **Search.** Wikimedia Commons was searched by feature, for example "Himeji Castle main keep", "Kyoto machiya
   facade lattice" and "Georgian door fanlight Dublin". Only bitmaps at least 900 px wide were kept, and every file's
   author and licence were recorded.
2. **Study.** Contact boards were made, and the useful images were studied at three scales:
   - macro: silhouette and proportions;
   - meso: the parts and how they attach;
   - micro: tiles, joints and shadows.
3. **Measure.** Proportions were measured in pixels, only on the public-domain and CC0 images.
4. **Rights.** No image is copied, traced or committed. Images under CC BY and CC BY-SA were looked at for how the
   buildings are made, never reproduced. The elevations are original drawings built from the rules.

## Observations

### Castle keep: Himeji's main keep, measured (himeji_0, 960 × 1285 px)

**Proportions**

- Above its stone base the keep is 548 px tall, and its first storey is 515 px wide. **The keep is about as tall as it
  is wide (1.06).** The old model was twice as tall as wide.
- Storey widths are 515, 490, about 435, 330 and 255 px. **Relative to the first storey: 1, 0.95, 0.86, 0.66, 0.5.**
  They narrow slowly at first, then quickly.
- Each tier (a wall plus its roof) is about a fifth of the base width.
- A roof tier shows 30–50 px above walls of 45–68 px, so **the roof is about 0.6–1.0 times its wall**.
- The eaves overhang by 35–45 px each side, which is 0.07–0.09 of the base width.
- The top roof is about 1.6 times its wall. Its ridge, between the shachihoko, is about 0.7 times its eave.

**Gables**

- The second roof carries a **kara-hafu**: a broad bell-shaped arch over a lattice bay, about 0.4 of the storey's width.
- The third roof carries **two chidori-hafu**, each about 0.4 of its storey, nearly touching.
- The fourth roof carries **one larger chidori-hafu**, about 0.5 of its storey.
- **The gable peaks rise in front of the wall of the storey above.**
- The barge-boards are white plaster under tiled rakes, with a small hanging ornament (gegyo) at each apex.

**Details**

- Windows are small, in pairs, with two or three bars each.
- The eave undersides are plastered white.
- White mortar on the tile joints makes the roofs read light grey from a distance.
- A small onigawara stands at every lifted corner, with hip ridges running up from the corners.

**Stone base (ishigaki)**

- Large irregular stones.
- The face is upright at the top and flares out toward the foot.
- At the corners, long and short stones alternate (sangi-zumi).

### Machiya: Kitagawa Morisada's drawings (machiya_0, machiya_2; public domain)

- The upper storey is low.
- It has one or two long **mushiko windows with rounded ends**, filled with plastered slats.
- A full-width tiled pent roof (hisashi) runs at the first floor, its tiles in wavy rows.
- The ground floor is a tall lattice over a solid board.
- Plastered fire walls (**udatsu**) stand at the party walls.

### Temple hall: Tōshōdai-ji kondō (temple2_0; CC0)

- The roof is about as tall as the walls, with deep eaves.
- **Bracket sets stand on each column under a band of white wall.**
- Rafter ends show at the eave's edge.
- The building stands on a stone platform with steps.

### Georgian town house: Bedford Square and a Dublin door (observation only)

- Dark stock brick, with **flat arches of rubbed red brick** over the windows.
- **Six-over-six sashes**, six over nine on the tall first floor, set back in white frames.
- A stucco band at first-floor level.
- The door: six panels, a **semicircular fanlight with radiating bars**, engaged columns, and steps up to it.
- Area railings, a parapet, and chimney stacks.

### Half-timbered house: Stein am Rhein (observation only)

**Seen in the photographs**

- Stone ground floors with round-arched doors.
- Framed upper storeys with ribbon windows.
- **Oriels on corbels.**
- A framed gable to the street.

**Taken from the timber-framing literature, not from these photographs** (Wikipedia: Timber framing, Jettying)

- St Andrew's crosses and diamonds in the panels under the windows.
- The 'Mann' brace figure.
- Joist ends under the jetties.

### Greek Doric temple: the Parthenon (parthenon_0; CC0, mostly under scaffolding)

- Doric capitals made of a cushion-like echinus and a square abacus.
- Fluted shafts.
- Columns set close together.

Dimensions come from the published figures already in the data: columns 10.4 m tall and about 1.9 m across.

### Adobe house (adobe_0; observation only)

**Seen in the photograph**

- A flat roof behind a parapet.
- Heavy timber lintels over deep-set openings.

**Inferred from Pueblo Revival descriptions** (Wikipedia: Pueblo Revival architecture), not from this photograph

- Vigas, canales, a portal on zapata corbels.
- A stepped parapet with rounded corners.

### Minka (gassho_0, gassho_1; observation only)

**Seen in the photographs**

- A thick thatch with a clean-cut edge at the eaves.
- Straw running down the slope.
- Paper shoji under the eaves.

**Inferred from minka descriptions**, not from these photographs

- Foundation stones under the posts.
- Saddle bundles (umanori) astride the ridge.

## Sciagraphy

Architects shade elevations by projecting each shadow from the depth of the part that casts it. ARCH1 now does the
same, with a light a little higher than the drafting convention's 45°:

- **Eaves and cornices:** a shadow falls 0.75 of the eave's projection down the wall and 0.55 of it across.
- **Windows and doors:** a recess shades its own head and left jamb.
- **Jetties, balconies, porches, oriels and vigas:** each casts its shadow in the same way.

Shadows are separate `cast` parts. A painter can drop them for a flat style.

## Remaining gaps

**Castle keep**

- Window placement is regular; Himeji's varies.
- No stone-drop openings (ishiotoshi) and no attached turrets.
- The stone courses are more regular than Himeji's rough-laid stone.

**Half-timbered house**

- No carving.
- No painted façades of the Stein am Rhein kind.

**Georgian town house**

- No mansard dormers.
- No basement area seen through the railings.

**Greek temple**

- No mutules and no pediment sculpture.
- The acroteria are generic palmettes.

**Adobe house and apartment block**

- The adobe has no photographic reference for the vigas and canales.
- The apartment block has no photographic reference at all.

**Review**

- No human visual review is recorded.

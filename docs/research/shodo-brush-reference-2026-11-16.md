## Observations

**Handle (軸).**
- Natural cane, pale straw to honey: the measured median colour of lit handles is about RGB 210/175/130.
- The surface is smooth, with a satin sheen along the lit side and fine darker specks. There is no large mottling.
- Many handles are cut between nodes, or they show one quiet node.
- The top end is a clean cut, sometimes with a cap and a cord loop for hanging.
- The handle is about as thick as the base of the tuft. On a 大筆 the handle is about three to four times the tuft length.

**Joint (首, 口巻き).**
- On Nara brushes, the hair goes straight into the cane.
- Many school and shop 大筆 have a short dark collar (black lacquer or plastic) at the joint. The collar is flush with the cane, not wider.
- No open end of a tube shows: the hair fills the cane.

**Tuft (穂).**
- New and dry, the tuft is white (goat) or tan to brown (horse, weasel). The hair is stiffened to a sharp point (命毛).
- The belly (腹) is only a little wider than the base (腰). It is about a third of the tuft length out from the joint.
- An inked brush is black and glossy from the point to about three quarters of the tuft.
- The quarter at the joint stays dry and pale. The edge between the two is soft, with single hairs that wick ink upward.
- Wet hair holds one smooth point. Its highlight is a soft band, not a hard line.

**Light and shadow.** The handle casts a long, soft shadow. Near the paper, the tuft's shadow is sharp.

## What changed in the drawing

- The end silhouettes of every cylinder (the handle and the collar) now use the outer half of the end ellipse. Before, they used the inner half, so a pale crescent and a black ellipse showed at the joint, as on an open tube.
- The collar is flush with the cane and short (0.42 of the tuft diameter). It is drawn before the handle, so the handle covers it toward a camera above the paper.
- The ink line on the hair starts at 22 % to 24 % of the tuft length when the brush is full. The dry base is pale, and single hairs have less contrast there. A short run between the dry and the wet part gives a wicking edge.
- The wet sheen is softer (a weaker thin core).
- The mottle of the scanned cane is kept at 60 % of the scan.
- The lean, the press, the bend and the motion of the brush are as before. They come from `shodoBrushPose`.

## Limits

- The brush is still code-drawn: there is no hair simulation and no subsurface light.
- The collar is always drawn, although Nara brushes have none.
- A human comparison at normal playback speed is still necessary.

### Observations

**Handle.**
- Pale cane shows very fine, straight lengthwise fibres. It has a slow change of tone along its length and only a few small specks (R1, R6).
- A node swells the cane about 5 % over about one diameter, with a raised ridge, a thin dark groove and a darker band (R3).
- The sheen is a soft satin band that the grain breaks up.
- The top is closed by a short black cap with a groove ring, sometimes with a cord loop (R3).

**Collar.**
- On school 大筆 the collar is long, about 1.5 to 2.5 handle diameters, black and satin. It has one broad soft grey band, not a mirror line (R1, R2).
- It is about as wide as the handle and tapers slightly toward the hair.
- A thin pale rim catches the light at the mouth.

**Tuft.**
- The hair leaves the collar slightly narrower than the collar (R2, R4).
- **Inked tuft:** charcoal, not ink black. Short broken light dashes follow the strands, each about 5 % to 15 % of the tuft length (R2).
- **Where the ink stops:** the boundary is interleaved like the teeth of a comb. Single dark strands reach into the dry base, and single white strands reach into the soaked part (R1, R2).
- **Dry base:** translucent cream, with grey between the hairs (R4).
- **Flyaways:** two to four single hairs stand out of the base at an angle (R1, R5).
- **Outline and point:** the outline is soft and bumpy where clumps bulge, and the point ends ragged, with a few hairs forking away (R2).

### What changed in the drawing

- **Handle:** a procedural cane replaces the scanned cane strip. The strip's fibres ran across the cane, and its mottle showed as blotches.
  - The new cane has long fibre bands and finer fibres along the length, a slow change of tone, and a few small elongated specks.
  - A multiply pass of the same grain breaks the sheen.
  - The node is at 58 % of the handle, with the swelling, groove, ridge and band.
  - The scan (ambientCG Bamboo001B) and its asset-manifest entry are removed.
- **Collar:** about 1.6 handle diameters long, 1.0 to 1.04 of the handle radius. It is satin lacquer with a soft band and a faint line, has a pale rim at the mouth, and its top face shows as a step around the cane.
- **End cap:** a black cap with a groove ring closes the top of the handle.
- **Tuft:**
  - Strand clumps are soft strokes, each with its own ink line, so dry and soaked hair interleave. Each hair's ink line spreads more widely.
  - Each hair varies in tone, and the outer hairs end short of the point.
  - Some tip hairs fork. The clumps bulge on their own, which makes the outline bumpy.
  - Two to four flyaways come out of the base.
  - Soaked hair reads charcoal: the sheen goes toward white, so a coloured ink keeps its hue.
  - Short smoothed highlight dashes along the strands replace the single blurred sheen streak.
- **Shadow:** the tuft's cast shadow is one filled shape. Before, overlapping round segments added their alphas into visible rings.
- **Outline:** at the knee, the tuft's outline takes its side direction over three samples, so the two sides no longer cross into spikes.

### Limits

- The brush is still code-drawn: there is no hair simulation and no light passing through the hair. The part lying on the paper reads as a dense dark mass, not as single hairs.
- There is no engraved lettering on the handle, which many real brushes have.
- Only the owner's review decides whether it reads as a real brush at playback speed.

### Owner review: the knee (2026-11-21)

The owner asked for a smoother join between the hair pressed onto the paper and the free hair above it. At the knee:

- each sample was either a round bundle or a flat fan, so hairs jumped from one place to the other and stuck out as spikes;
- the curve from the collar ended on the paper, while the lying part started 0.04 D above it;
- the bundle's thickness dropped to a third in one step.

Now:

- The bundle flattens gradually, from six samples before the knee to two after it. Each hair moves from its place in the round bundle to its place in the fan.
- The curve from the collar ends at the height of the lying part.
- The thickness falls over the first quarter of the lying part.
- Three relaxation passes, weighted around the knee, round off its corner. The knee is still sharper under a harder press, as in the side-view study.


## Third study: the pressed part (2026-11-21)

The part of the tuft that lies on the paper read as a smooth grey wedge, and a few hairs crossed at the knee. This study looked at the teacher's own lesson videos at 1080p:

- [「大」の書き方とコツ](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=J2tJlU0NwgQ): V14 in the [calligraphy reference study](shodo-calligraphy-reference-study-2026-11-15.md).
- [「永」の書き方とコツ](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=afB6vG44CnI): V3 in that study.

Frames were taken at 2 fps over the whole 大, and at 10 fps over 3.5 s from 7.6 s, where the brush lands and presses. As before, no frame is in the repository and nothing was traced.

### Observations

- **Loaded, in the air:** dark grey, with small light flecks. The point is sharp.
- **Pressed:** the tuft becomes one smooth black mass, as black as the stroke under it. No single hair shows: ink fills the gaps between the hairs. The front is round, not pointed.
- **Gloss:** the light breaks into many small white and blue-grey flecks in a band along the lit side of the pressed mass. The band thins out toward the knee. The side away from the light stays black.
- **Fresh ink:** where it lies thick, a fresh stroke shows blue-grey reflections of the room.

### Diagnosis of the crossing hairs

- **Twisted frames:** the old frames along the tuft twisted through the knee. Near a vertical handle, they also switched their reference, so the frames jumped as the lean changed.
- **Mismatched fan:** the fan on the paper used its own side direction, not the frame of the round bundle. Where the two disagreed, a hair moved from one side of the tuft to the other through the knee.
- **Long way round:** a hair drawn toward its clump turned the long way round the tuft when the two angles were on either side of zero, so it swung through the far side.

### What changed in the drawing

- **Frames:** rotation-minimizing frames (double reflection), from a reference fixed at the collar. They do not twist through the knee and do not jump with the lean.
- **Flattening:** the round bundle is pressed flat. Each hair keeps its place across the stroke, scaled out to the contact width, and keeps its height, squashed. The lower half lies on the paper. No hair changes sides through the knee.
- **Clump angles:** a hair turns toward its clump by the short way round.
- **Draw order:** hairs and clumps are drawn back to front by their offset toward the camera, not by a fixed angle.
- **Pressed outline:** the outline of the pressed hair has the same shape as the ink's contact patch: blunter under a harder press (`shodoContactTipPower`, shared by the brush and the ink).
- **Wet film:** a near-black film covers the pressed hair. It fades in from where the hair is half pressed and reaches a little past the outermost hairs, for a crisp edge. It begins below the knee because, higher up, the knee can fold back over the pressed part on screen.
- **Gloss:**
  - The specular comes from the normal of the pressed surface on a grid. Normals are taken over two samples each way, so the sharp turn of the knee does not switch a whole row off at once. The lobe is tight (exponent 70), because wet ink is glossy.
  - Each grid cell holds up to ten specks at fixed places. The brighter the cell, the more specks show. Each speck takes its brightness from the four corners of its cell.
  - Faint specks are blue-grey and the strongest are white. A soft blue-grey sheen, blurred over about a cell, lies under them.
  - The gloss fades in over the knee more gradually than the film, because the bent hair is wet too.
  - All values follow the pose smoothly, so the gloss glints and fades but does not flicker.
- **Side view:** the highlights of the hairs use the side camera's own view direction.

### Cost

On the CPU, with no raster, one draw in contact takes 2.1 ms. Before, it took 1.8 ms. In headless Chromium at 2× it takes 2.2 ms, with the raster.

### Limits

- The camera looks straight down, so the gloss shows only where the pressed surface turns toward the light. With the teacher's oblique camera the band is wider and brighter.
- In fast moves, the multi-pose motion blur shows each speck as a few copies, not as one streak.
- Only the owner's review decides whether the pressed tuft reads as wet ink at playback speed.

### Technique and process authorities

- [People of Print — Mayumi Tsuzuki](https://peopleofprint.com/disciplines/illustration/mayumi-tsuzuki/): separately drawn pencil-gradient grayscale plates translated to Risograph color separations. Real tonal richness is not equivalent to applying whole-image alpha.
- [University of Illinois — Riso printing and halftone guide](https://web.faa.illinois.edu/app/uploads/sites/6/2021/05/riso_print_guide.pdf): screen frequency and angle are per-layer production variables; do not replace structural anatomy with uniform dot noise.
- [Natalie Andrewson — Makers Series](https://www.natalieandrewson.com/products/6-makers-series-risograph-prints): prints are made one separate stencil/ink at a time, with natural bounded registration variation. Frame-to-frame random registration is a defect in animation.

## What our RISO11 Courier still lacks

The existing paper knockout now prevents coat/arms from darkening the face, but it does not confer realistic artistic anatomy. The current portrait, coat and glove paths are largely front-facing and in one illustrator's fixed proportions. A full actor requires:

1. **Face construction, not symbols:** eye-socket, upper/lower lid, iris edge/eyelid cover, nasal bridge/alar planes, mouth corners/orbicular lip, chin/jaw, ear/temple and directional hair roots. Each has at least neutral / speaking / smiling / blink landmarks derived from one original design lock. Current one-patch iris and triangular nose are too schematic for the chosen high-detail direction.
2. **Hand anatomy and true grip:** wrist->palm->metacarpals->three finger joints->fingertips plus thumb opposition, negative gaps and contact points. The source glove is still effectively a single red mitt shape; painted creases cannot substitute for knuckle geometry, separated silhouette and grasp.
3. **Garment drape and volume:** coat silhouette based on shoulder/neck/chest/waist/pelvis, sleeve creases anchored at joint stress, lapel overlap, fabric thickness at seams, asymmetric hem tension, coherent overprint shadows. Do not draw equally spaced random folds.
4. **Pose variation:** reference the true side, 3/4 and rear projections before adding drawings; no made-up back views from the front cutout. Confirm limb scale and anatomical reach through all actions.
5. **Print medium:** paper as the lightest face tone and negative-space layer, one dominant ink per structure, localized secondary screen tone only where physical depth needs it; stop broad dark blue-on-red face shadow from hiding expression.
6. **Motion:** full-speed viewing of gesture change and weight transfer, not proof from a few stills; moving hands require contact constraints, changing finger separation, and occlusion ordering as wrist angle changes.

## Next independent code deliverables — ordered by visual benefit

**P0 — one original Courier portrait that reads as an intentional illustrated person.** Create a source-backed character sheet with measurable landmarks and *approved* neutral, blink, speech, smile and three-quarter head studies. Replace facial symbols by layered hand-authored shapes. Capture unstyled geometry, mono-ink, two-ink and production scale at the same poses. Preserve face paper clearance.

**P1 — articulate one hand.** Build thumb and at least three independent finger masses with pivot/knuckle landmarks, then add glove seam and tapered crease contours *after* the grasp silhouette passes. Prove right-hand-to-satchel grip in walking and arm-reach poses; no floating digits or penetration into props.

**P2 — tailor the coat.** Build shoulder-to-elbow tension maps, lapel/neck overlap, cuff topology and hem pleats that change coherently with the rig. Preserve the full-body silhouette under fast motion, portrait framing and 2-ink isolation.

**P3 — add a small actor ensemble.** Use original characters designed with diverse proportions and garments; pose them waving, holding, passing objects and embracing. Borrow Mina Braun / Amy Matsushita-Beal's *construction lessons*, never their distinctive actual people, faces or layouts.

**P4 — print material calibration.** Use the physically separable plate pipeline; add reference-specific dot density, solid breakups, registration trap, paper/ink scaling, and color-negative cutouts only after the silhouette and expression pass.

## ArtCraft review gates

- Create an original source sheet and study/structure manifests for each actor. Label source references and copyrights. Do not claim a lookalike or directly trace a marketed artist print.
- Per semantic crop, use the five axes (topology / flow / stroke pressure / optical rhythm / microtexture), but establish thresholds only after source-specific masks and evidence. A flat-grayscale threshold cannot identify all anatomical parts.
- **Face:** verify landmarks and negative space at 128px thumbnail and 3x zoom; compare neutral/blink/speech/smile and 3/4, including both ink plates and paper-only clearance.
- **Hands:** connected-component and contact-root checks for each digit, palm and thumb; silhouette edge separation, acceptable gaps and prop attachment verified after transforms at every stress pose.
- **Garments:** creases flow from anatomical seam roots and follow limb movement; at least three physically meaningful tension sites per garment region, not arbitrary visual noise.
- **Animation:** deterministic shuffled/forward/reverse frame seeking, 16:9 + 9:16, nonpenetrating body/prop contact and actual encoded full-speed MP4.
- **Approval:** automated checks passing is necessary but not sufficient; an independent visual reviewer must separately record defects and final acceptance.

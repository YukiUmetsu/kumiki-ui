## ANIM1 comparison

The project animal-anatomy lookup identifies `red-fox` as a digitigrade canid. Its typical construction uses a level back, a chest clearance of 0.52 shoulder heights and separate fore and hind leg chains; the fox trunk estimate is 0.95 shoulder heights. The fox record does not currently provide a face sheet or dedicated reference images, so those still require source study. The chest and clearance values are **plan-derived**, and the trunk range is **estimated**; they are guides rather than measured claims about this character or a particular fox.

For a preliminary check of the existing authored geometry, approximate rest-space landmarks were read from the paper paths: ground y=2, withers (0, −48), brisket (18, −12), shoulder (24, −37), buttock (−43, −33). `measureAnimal(..., false)` gives clearance 0.28, chest depth 0.72 and trunk 1.34; `checkAnimalProportions` flags all three outside the default 10% tolerance. The broad stylized contour does not mark anatomical landmarks, so these coordinates are provisional and must be checked on an annotated actual render before treating the numerical differences as precise. They nevertheless identify the short legs and deep torso as the first static construction issues, ahead of any gait work. Preserve the intentionally expressive paper shapes while making the anatomy and silhouette a deliberate, reviewable choice.

## Static construction study

[Istvan Gerenyi's standing fox photograph](https://www.pexels.com/photo/red-fox-in-rocky-mountain-habitat-31101019/) was inspected at its 5200 × 4160 source resolution; downloaded JPEG SHA-256 `34422067c63f9f82ca925b5a12ab426c1428b707caa24b6009d78f87d4ad63d9`. Pexels marks the photo free under its [license](https://www.pexels.com/license/); the photograph is linked for study and not redistributed. The lateral body reads as a long back flowing into a rounded rump, with an underside that rises behind the front leg. The two visible hind limbs have distinct, slender dark lower segments and a backward-set hock; the foreleg descends from the chest to a grounded dark paw. The low white throat connects visually to the cheek, and the full tail hangs from the rump with a small pale tip. Far-side feet overlap and the ground is uneven, so the photo does not determine hidden joints or a gait.

| Observed feature | Original cut-paper construction to test | Preserve / inspect |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Abdomen lifts between the limb roots | Raise the lower torso contour into a shallow tuck; keep a solid, continuous stock sheet | No detached flank or loss of the five separate fur cuts |
| Slender dark lower legs terminate in grounded paws | Reshape the existing two opaque limb sheets with narrower ankles and individually shaped paws | Keep their roots overlapped behind the torso; check silhouette on light and dark backing |
| Fore chest and white throat flow together | Shorten the white chest sheet with the raised belly while retaining the cheek sheet | No white sliver floating below the body or occluding the foreleg |
| Back, head and tail have distinct mass and material | Retain their authored contours, attachments and fine details | Inspect original and candidate at 1× and 3× in both formats; this is not a rig or gait claim |

### Trial result — rejected

A static experiment raised the belly and reshaped the two existing limb sheets while retaining the original sheet count. The [actual landscape comparison](assets/paper-royal-study/fox-static-trial2-rejected.png) shows the original at left and the trial at right, enlarged 3× with nearest-neighbor sampling (comparison SHA-256 `948f5f03664b04465526a68b93cb8ae5400353a7cc184470a41a3b0679d262df`). An independent reviewer rejected it: although the belly opening improved, both limbs still read as posts, the hind paw flares like a pedestal, and the chest flap hides the foreleg's attachment. The torso and limbs appear pasted together, with no meaningful far-side leg separation or grounding. This review covered only the landscape still. A portrait trial frame was rendered and inspected, but was not independently reviewed; neither still establishes motion quality. The experimental geometry was removed from production.

The next attempt needs a coherent silhouette and overlap design for the chest, foreleg, haunch, hock and paws together, instead of narrowing existing legs in isolation. Establish a polished stationary fox and its layer budget before introducing separate walking joints.

## Next construction decision

Find a permitted side-view reference with sufficiently visible feet and independently record support and swing intervals, then produce a source-informed part and pivot plan that preserves the fox's detailed silhouette and stock overlaps. Test foot position in scene coordinates through declared support intervals and inspect both aspect ratios at motion extremes and normal speed. Design a shared direction, readable spacing and a deliberate look or cue between the actors. The portrait scene already reaches the 128-layer limit, so resolve the layer budget without deleting detail or silently raising the limit. A staged still, the motion evidence, and independent visual review are required before treating the guidance beat as finished.

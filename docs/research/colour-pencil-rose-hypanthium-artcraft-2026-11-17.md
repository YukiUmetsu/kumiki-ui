## Actual-source observations and authorship

1. **K. R. Robertson, Illinois Natural History Survey, Rosa flower longitudinal section** — https://www.life.illinois.edu/help/digitalflowers/Flowers/56.htm . Photograph demonstrates Rosa's perianth/androecium attachment along a **continuous floral cup (hypanthium)** above a pedicel. The receptacular cup is not two floating leaves below the corolla.
2. **University of Minnesota, The Science of Plants: 7.2 Flower Morphology** — https://open.lib.umn.edu/horticulture/chapter/7-2-flower-morphology/ . Explains flower organ whorls' attachment to a floral axis. Do not mistake the prominent green sepals for a disconnected stiff "star" beside the petals.
3. **Native Plant Trust, Go Botany: Rosa key** — https://gobotany.nativeplanttrust.org/dkey/rosa/ . Sepals can be reflexed/spreading and the hypanthium varies by genus/cultivar; do not assume a perfect pentagram beneath every double rose.
4. **Leyth, original CC0 Rose Blossom Closeup (Wikimedia Commons)** — https://commons.wikimedia.org/wiki/File:Rose_Blossom_Closeup.jpg . Independent look at full-flower contact and basal green support, not a pixel/value target for this unregistered flower.
5. **Actual local source image**: the real native Chromium 960×540 full 45-region film after #615, plus source-native 2.15× from #614. The front-left and front-right laminae terminate near **y274–278**; original source `calyx-receptacle` starts at **y282–291** with visibly disconnected triangular ivory wedges between. This is a geometry/hierarchy gap. Darkening it with 30 pencil strokes would not make the flower anatomically joined.
